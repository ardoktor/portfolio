mulakat.pro

mulakat.pro generated mock interviews from a job description and a CV: it read both, worked out where the candidate fell short of the role, and turned that gap into an ordered preparation plan with voice-based practice. It was built by a small team for job candidates staring at a posting and not knowing what to study first. We stopped it the same year we launched it — people tried it once and didn't come back. What we actually built

The framing was the interesting part: not a bank of practice questions but gap analysis. The CV and the job post were parsed into structured signals — skills claimed, skills required — and the delta between them became the product: an ordered study plan, a readiness estimate, and mock interviews generated against the specific role rather than a generic template. Voice practice existed because the frightening part of an interview is speaking, not reading; a typed Q&A rehearses the wrong skill. All of it ran as controlled pipelines over the parsed documents, with evaluation and progress tracking behind an API.

What happened

First use worked. There was no second use.

The first session was the whole product experience: upload the CV, point at the job post, get the gap report and a mock interview. People completed it, and the ones we talked to were positive about the output. Then the logs went quiet. The product delivered its value in one sitting — once you've read your gap report, you have it. Coming back would have meant preparing for a different job, and most people weren't.

The retention features nobody needed

We had built for a returning user from the start: progress tracking, readiness re-scoring, the study plan as a thing you'd work through over weeks. That was the attempted fix, baked in early — give people a reason to come back and measure themselves. It didn't move anything, and the reason became obvious in hindsight: nobody wants to track progress on an event. They want the interview to be over.

A category problem, not a retention problem

The need was real — unlike MentAI, people arrived with a burning, specific problem. But interview preparation is intensely needed for about two weeks and then not at all. A product whose need doesn't recur has no retention problem to fix; it has a category problem, and no amount of iteration on our side could make interviews happen more often. That realization ended the project more than any metric did.

Why we stopped

Once we accepted that the need was one-shot, the options were to chase volume — a funnel business, not the product we wanted to build — or to stop. We stopped. The clearest evidence of how settled the decision was: when the mulakat.pro domain came up for renewal, we let it lapse. Letting it expire was a decision, not neglect.

What it changed

mulakat.pro added the test that MentAI hadn't taught us: does the need come back next week? ConsulDent is the direct answer — a dentist's questions recur every working day, inside a workflow she is already in, in a profession narrow enough to reach. Recurring need over one-time need, and an audience defined by their workday rather than by a life event. That filter came from here.
